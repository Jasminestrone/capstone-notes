# Andrew — Interview Notes
**Age:** 19
**Date:** 2026-04-09
**Profile:** Self-aware, has already acted on the problem — deleted Reels and YouTube Shorts. Likes boredom. Strong sense of identity and direction.

---

## Section 1: Warm-Up — Daily Life & Phones

Best-case morning: wakes up, gets immediately out of bed, checks the time on his phone with relief. The phone enters the picture right away, but as a clock — not a scroll.

Most disconnected from his phone during **basketball** (throws it aside so it doesn't fall out of his pocket or get knocked around) and when playing **video games** he's really into. Two very different modes of absorption — one physical, one digital — but both crowd the phone out entirely.

---

## Section 2: Boredom — Personal Experience

Andrew said something unexpected: **he likes boredom.** For him, boredom is the absence of stimulation that makes room for something else — he paces around his house and thinks about games and projects he wants to make. It doesn't feel bad; it feels generative.

Last time he was in that state was **yesterday** — he's been "rawdogging" the train ride, meaning he deliberately doesn't use his phone on the T anymore. He sits and thinks about what he's going to do at school.

When boredom hits, the **reflex is still to whip out his phone** — but he's been actively working against that. He said his brain has been "almost rewired" for short-form content, and fighting it takes real effort.

He doesn't frame boredom as unpleasant. It's more like idle time he's learned to protect.

---

## Section 3: Idle Mind — Creativity, Reflection, Purpose

His mind is most free when he's **pacing and thinking about things he wants to make**. That's the state where he feels like himself — not reactive, just generative. He also mentioned that **talking to people about projects** produces some of his best ideas, even beyond solo pacing.

On existential thinking — where is his life going, who is he — he pushed back on the premise. He said he thinks about that *a lot* and feels fortunate to already know who he is and what he wants to make. He didn't feel like phone use had taken that away from him.

---

## Section 4: Existing Solutions — What's Worked and What Hasn't

He deleted Instagram Reels because it was **eating time he actually wanted back** — not because of grades or productivity metrics, but because he wasn't doing the things he actually enjoyed. "It's just making me miserable." He couldn't experience anything fully.

He described the difficulty with short-form content as the way **it reinvents itself** — the algorithm adapts to whatever interest you have, so there's no clean break. It doesn't feel like one addiction; it feels like a shapeshifter. Since deleting Reels and YouTube Shorts, things have gotten better, but he acknowledged the underlying mechanism is still there.

He didn't comment on screen time apps directly, but his lived approach — full deletion rather than restriction — implies he's skeptical that partial measures work.

## Post-Interview Notes

**Strongest moment:** His immediate "I like boredom" — said without hesitation. That's an unusual and useful data point. Most interviewees treat boredom as the problem; Andrew treats it as the goal.

**Tension worth noting:** He says his brain has been rewired and the reflex to grab his phone is still there — even though he's acting against it. That gap between intention and reflex is the real design space.

**Quote worth keeping:** *"It's not even like I wasn't doing good in school — it's just making me miserable. Like, I don't even get to experience anything."*

**Quote worth keeping:** *"It reinvents itself."* — on why short-form content is so hard to quit. The algorithm adapts to you, so deleting one app isn't enough; something else fills the niche.

**Language to use:** "rawdogging" as a self-imposed phone-free mode. He's normalized this enough to have a word for it. Could be a useful frame for the project.

**Contradiction:** He says he's figured out who he is and doesn't feel existentially unmoored — but he also deleted Reels specifically because he wasn't doing the things he loved. Those aren't quite the same thing. The loss was less about identity and more about *time and enjoyment*.
