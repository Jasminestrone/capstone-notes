# Munther — Interview Template
**Age:** 17
**Profile:** Boredom very uncomfortable · Also believes boredom to be "useful or creative" · Has tried many interventions (dumb phone, app blockers, habit techniques) · Can speak to why each failed

- Record with permission
- Let him lead
- Avoid leading questions. If he goes somewhere unexpected, follow it.
- You don't have to ask every question.
- *He's the most reflective candidate — he'll likely have opinions. Let him. But push on the gap between knowing and changing.*

---

## Section 1: Warm-Up — Daily Life & Phones

1. Walk me through a typical morning — from when you wake up to when you leave the house. What role does your phone play in that?

2. Are there moments in your day where you're not looking at a screen? What are those like?

3. When do you feel most "checked out" from your phone — and what makes those moments possible?

---

## Section 2: Boredom — Personal Experience

4. What does boredom feel like for you? Can you describe it physically or emotionally?

5. When was the last time you were actually bored — like, genuinely without anything to do? What happened?

6. What do you do in those moments? Is there a reflex?

7. Have you ever sat with boredom and not escaped it? What happened if you did? *(Follow up: what did that feel like in your body — any sensory details, where you felt it?)*

8. You've described boredom as uncomfortable, but also as something useful or creative — how do those two things coexist for you? *(This is the interesting tension for him — don't rush past it.)*

---

## Section 3: Idle Mind — Creativity, Reflection, Purpose

9. When do you feel like your mind is most free — like you're actually thinking your own thoughts, not reacting to something?

10. Do you have places or situations where you tend to have good ideas, or work things out emotionally? What are those like?

# 11. Do you ever feel like you're missing time to think about bigger things — like where your life is going, what you want, who you are? Has that ever felt connected to your phone use?

12. Have you ever noticed a difference in how you feel on days when you've spent less time on your phone?

---

## Section 4: Existing Solutions — What's Worked and What Hasn't

# 13. You've tried a lot of things to change your relationship with your phone. What first made you want to?

# 14. Walk me through what you actually tried — the dumb phone, app blockers, habit techniques. What did each look like in practice?

# 15. For each one: what happened over time? Where did it break down? *(He can speak to why each failed — let him go through it. These are your richest data points.)*

# 16. What do you think is the real reason it's hard to change phone habits — even when you understand the problem and you're actively trying? *(He's in a unique position to answer this honestly.)*

17. Do you think screen time apps or timers actually help people? Why or why not?

---

## Section 5: Concept Reaction — The Device

*Introduce the project.*

18. What's your gut reaction to that?

19. Does the problem it's solving feel real to you personally?

20. What would make you actually reach for something like that instead of your phone? *(He's tried a lot — what hasn't existed yet?)*

21. What would make you not bother with it? *(Pressure test: what would make this fail like the other things he's tried?)*

22. Is there a version of this that would feel genuinely compelling — not just useful in theory, but something you'd actually want?

---

## Closing

# 23. Is there anything I didn't ask about that feels relevant — about boredom, your phone, or what kind of device might actually help?

**Thank him.** Let him know how the research will be used and whether/how he'll be credited.

---

## Post-Interview Notes
- Any surprising or unexpected answers
- Moments of strong emotion or resonance
- Specific quotes worth preserving verbatim
- Contradictions between what he said he does vs. what he actually described doing
- Any language he used that might be worth incorporating into the project framing
