# Noelle — Interview Template
**Age:** 19
**Profile:** Almost never has mental space to think · Finds boredom very uncomfortable · Phone separation anxiety · 4–6 hrs screen time daily

- Record with permission
- Let her lead
- Avoid leading questions. If she goes somewhere unexpected, follow it.
- You don't have to ask every question.

---

## Section 1: Warm-Up — Daily Life & Phones

1. Walk me through a typical morning — from when you wake up to when you leave the house. What role does your phone play in that? *(She's described as always mentally occupied — let her paint the picture herself.)*

2. Are there moments in your day where you're not looking at a screen? What are those like?

3. When do you feel most "checked out" from your phone — and what makes those moments possible? *(Given the phone separation anxiety, this might be rare — notice if she struggles to name one.)*

---

## Section 2: Boredom — Personal Experience

4. What does boredom feel like for you? Can you describe it physically or emotionally?

5. When was the last time you were actually bored — like, genuinely without anything to do? What happened? *(She's almost never without mental stimulation — if she draws a blank, that's worth sitting with.)*

6. What do you do in those moments? Is there a reflex?

7. Have you ever sat with boredom and not escaped it? What happened if you did? *(Follow up: what did that feel like in your body? Any sensory details — sounds, restlessness, where you felt it?)*

8. Do you think of boredom as a bad thing, a good thing, or something else?

---

## Section 3: Idle Mind — Creativity, Reflection, Purpose

9. When do you feel like your mind is most free — like you're actually thinking your own thoughts, not reacting to something?

10. Do you have places or situations where you tend to have good ideas, or work things out emotionally? What are those like?

11. You mentioned almost never having mental space to think. Does that feel like a loss? Has it ever felt connected to your phone? *(She already named this — invite her to go deeper.)*

12. Have you ever noticed a difference in how you feel on days when you've spent less time on your phone?

---

## Section 4: Existing Solutions — What's Worked and What Hasn't

13. Have you ever tried to cut back on your phone use? What made you want to? *(If she brings up the anxiety around being without her phone, ask her to describe what that actually feels like.)*

14. What did you actually try — apps, settings, leaving your phone in another room, anything?

15. Did it work? What happened over time?

16. What do you think is the real reason it's hard to change phone habits? *(Not the obvious answer — the real one.)*

17. Do you think screen time apps or timers actually help people? Why or why not?

---

## Section 5: Concept Reaction — The Device

*Introduce the project.*

18. What's your gut reaction to that?

19. Does the problem it's solving feel real to you personally?

20. What would make you actually reach for something like that instead of your phone? *(For someone with separation anxiety around her phone, what would it need to offer?)*

21. What would make you not bother with it?

22. Is there a version of this that would feel genuinely compelling — not just useful in theory, but something you'd actually want?

---

## Closing

23. Is there anything I didn't ask about that feels relevant — about boredom, your phone, or what kind of device might actually help?

**Thank her.** Let her know how the research will be used and whether/how she'll be credited.

---

## Post-Interview Notes
- Any surprising or unexpected answers
- Moments of strong emotion or resonance
- Specific quotes worth preserving verbatim
- Contradictions between what she said she does vs. what she actually described doing
- Any language she used that might be worth incorporating into the project framing
