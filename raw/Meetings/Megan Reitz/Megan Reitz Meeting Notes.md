 Her core framework: two modes of attention — doing mode (narrow, task-focused, controlling, efficient) and spacious mode (expansive, unhurried, open to relationships and possibility). The problem isn't that we work hard, it's that we never give ourselves permission to leave doing mode. Her current book is specifically about spaciousness and the permission to pause.


She spoke about things called "speed bumps" that could just be a post-it note that would help re orient people

S - Safety, feeling psychologically safe to pause and not be "on", if you fear being judged for being unproductive or feel you'll fall behind, you stay trapped in doing mode
P - People, having people you're close to and friendly with hanging out with them makes you not be on your phone and puts you into a healthy spacious mindset
A - Attention, being intentional about where you direct your attention rather than letting tasks and notifications capture it for you — actively choosing to step back into a wider awareness
C - Conflict, high conflict or urgency keeps you reactive and locked in doing mode; reducing unnecessary tension (internal or external) is what makes spacious mode accessible
E - Environment, having cluttered space or having your phone on your desk leads to more distraction
