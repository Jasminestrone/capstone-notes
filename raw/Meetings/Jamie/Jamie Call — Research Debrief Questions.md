# Jamie Call — Research Debrief Questions
*April 2026 · Follow-up after survey + interviews*

---

1. **You mentioned that before COVID, not every student even had a phone on them — and that the groups from those years bonded differently.** Can you describe what downtime actually looked like then? What were students doing in the unstructured gaps between activities?
> *Notes:* 

2. **When phones were allowed in the program for a year or two, you said it created a barrier to communication and stunted relationship formation.** What did that look like in practice — like, what specific moment made you realize it was a problem?
> *Notes:*

3. **You said students now need more hand-holding on independent assignments than they used to.** Is that more about focus — like they can't sustain attention — or is it more that they don't know what to do when there's no clear next step? I'm trying to figure out if it's a distraction problem or a tolerance-for-ambiguity problem.
> *Notes:*

4. **My research found that the only phone-free moments people report are the shower and right before sleep — neither of which they chose.** In your program, are there moments that accidentally create that kind of space — where students are just stuck being present with no device and no task? And what happens in those moments?
> *Notes:*

5. **You said the key loss is students' ability to overcome social nerves and meet new people, which used to happen naturally in downtime.** What does a student look like who hasn't had to do that? When you watch them try to connect without their phone as a buffer, what do you actually see?
> *Notes:*

6. **One of my interviewees described his phone as giving him "a sense of purpose when he can't find one."** Do you see that in your students — using the phone less as entertainment and more as something to hold onto when they don't know what to do with themselves?
> *Notes:*

7. **Your program runs the same students across three summers.** Do you see a shift in the same kid over time — either getting more embedded in their phone, or finding their footing without it? What seems to cause one or the other?
> *Notes:*

8. **You mentioned that the COVID cohorts struggled more to bond outside of structured activities.** Do you think those students ever caught up — like, did the following summers normalize for them — or did that cohort carry the deficit forward?
> *Notes:*

9. **My research keeps pointing to the social dimension as the hardest part to solve — individual behavior change fails when the social environment hasn't changed.** Your program is one of the few contexts I can think of where you actually have control over a group's social environment for a sustained period. Have you ever tried anything explicitly around that — like making phone-free time a group norm rather than a personal rule?
> *Notes:*

10. **I'm doing a small pilot study at the end of this project — 5 to 8 people using a prototype for a few days.** If you could put it in front of one of your students, what kind of student would you pick? Who do you think would actually engage with something designed to make doing nothing feel worth doing?
> *Notes:*

11. **You mentioned the program runs the same cohort across three summers — so you're watching students at 14, 15, and 16.** Is there a moment in that arc where a student stops needing the phone as a social crutch in the program? Like, does it get easier for them to just be present as they get older and more comfortable? Or does it go the other direction?
> *Notes:*

12. **What do students actually say when they talk about their phones?** Not what you observe — but what they say out loud. Do they joke about being addicted, do they defend it, do they not mention it at all? I'm curious whether they have language for it or if it's just invisible to them.
> *Notes:*

13. **What does the first night of the program look like?** I'm thinking about whether there's something like a withdrawal period — students who are restless, can't settle, don't know what to do with themselves — before the program's rhythm takes over.
> *Notes:*

14. **Are there activities in the program that actually compete successfully with phones — like, things students get absorbed in enough that the phone stops being the pull?** And if so, what do those activities have in common?
> *Notes:*

15. **Do parents texting and calling throughout the program pull students back in?** I'm wondering if the social tether is partly coming from home — the expectation of being reachable — not just from peers.
> *Notes:*
