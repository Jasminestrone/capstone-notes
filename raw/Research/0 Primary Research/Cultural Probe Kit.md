# Cultural Probe Kit — Boredom & Idle Time
*For participants. Duration: 5–7 days. ~2 minutes per day.*

---

## What This Is

This isn't a survey. There are no right answers. I'm trying to understand what boredom and idle time actually feel like in your daily life — in your own words, in the moment, not in retrospect.

All I'm asking is that you respond to a short daily prompt and take one photo or voice memo per day. That's it.

---

## Daily Prompts
*Sent each morning. Respond whenever feels natural that day.*

**Day 1 — The reflex**
Think back to the last time you had a gap with nothing to do — waiting for something, sitting alone, a moment between tasks. What did you do first? Describe it like you're telling a friend.

**Day 2 — The phone**
At some point today, notice a moment when you reach for your phone out of habit — not because you need something specific. When it happens, write down: where you are, what you were doing before, and what you were hoping to find.

**Day 3 — Capture**
At some point today, take a photo of a place or moment where your mind tends to wander — a specific chair, a commute, a spot outside. Don't explain it. Just send the photo.

**Day 4 — Discomfort**
Have you felt bored today? If yes: describe what it felt like physically or emotionally. If no: why do you think you haven't?

**Day 5 — The last good idea**
When did you last have a thought that felt genuinely yours — an idea, a realization, something you figured out about yourself or your life? Where were you? What were you doing?

**Day 6 — The alternative**
If your phone disappeared for a day, what would you actually do with the time? Be honest — not what you think you should do, what you'd actually do.

**Day 7 — Looking back**
Looking at this past week: when did your mind feel most free? What were the conditions — where you were, who you were with, what you were (or weren't) doing?

---

## How to Respond

- Text or voice memo is fine — whatever's easiest
- Don't overthink it. First instinct is better than a polished answer
- Photos don't need captions unless you want to add one
- If a prompt doesn't resonate, just say so — that's useful too

---

## Setup Instructions (for researcher)

**Recruiting:** 4–6 participants, ideally Gen Z (18–27). People you can follow up with in a short interview afterward. Friends, classmates, or acquaintances work — you don't need strangers.

**Delivery method (pick one):**
- iMessage/GroupMe group → send prompts manually each morning
- Google Form with one question per day → share a new link each morning
- Notion page → participants log responses directly

**Timing:** Send prompts between 8–10am. Participants respond whenever — morning, evening, whenever it happens naturally.

**What to collect:**
- Text responses to each prompt
- Photos from Day 3 (and any other days they capture something)
- Note any responses that surprise you, contradict the lit review, or feel emotionally charged — these are your best interview leads

**Follow-up interviews:** After Day 7, schedule a 20–30 min conversation with 2–3 of the most interesting respondents using the Interview Guide. Use their probe responses as jumping-off points — "on Day 4 you said X, tell me more about that."

---

## What You're Looking For

- Language people use to describe boredom (not your language — theirs)
- Situations where idle time happens naturally vs. situations where it's impossible
- What the phone is actually replacing — the emotional/cognitive need, not just the activity
- Any evidence of the DMN at work: moments of insight, creativity, or self-reflection that happened during unstructured time
- Contradictions: people who say they hate being bored but describe their best ideas happening in idle moments
