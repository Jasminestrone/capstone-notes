Two types of Galvantic Vestibular Stimulation (GVS) exist, GVS and Noisy Galvantic vestibular stimulation (nGVS). GVS is a technique in which low amperage electrical currents are sent slightly above and behind your ear into the mastoid bones to affect your vestibular system. Noisy galvantic vestibular stimulation (nGVS) has been shown to reduce postural sway, With nGVS, subjects maintaned a more "erect stance," and followed the displacement of their support-surfaces closer. Based off of this, it could be proposed that you could enhance a users posture via nGVS. This research was conducted in 2000 however was reaffirmed in 2020 where it was tested on both patients with bilateral vestibular hypofunction as well as healthy patients where it was shown to both assist in balance while standing, as well as head yaw movements while walking. Changes were measured in electrical activity in both the left and right precentral gyrus as well as the right parietal lobe during as well as after nGVS was introduced.
